Where this is really useful

Any place where two sides need to find each other and neither wants to hand over its list first.

HIRING A SPECIALIST

A studio and a provider

A brand studio needs infrastructure work. The provider has three teams free. Neither publishes its client list or its pipeline. They learn they overlap on two things, and only after both sides' people agree.

SUPPLY CHAINS

A buyer and a supplier

A buyer will not post its requirements to a public index, and a supplier will not list its capacity. They match on declared capability, region and compliance marks, and keep prices and volumes out of the exchange entirely.

SKILLS AND SERVICES

A marketplace that does not leak its catalogue

A marketplace wants to route paid work without publishing what each seller can do. The catalogue stays inside each seller's agent. Buyers get routing, sellers keep their lists.

PARTNERING

Two companies exploring a deal

Before the first meeting, each side wants to know whether the other is worth the trip. The engine answers that question without either side revealing its portfolio.

AGENT TRAFFIC

Agents that must transact on their own

When agents act between tasks, a signed card says who each agent is, the turn grammar keeps the talk bounded, and each step leaves evidence that can be checked afterwards.

COMPLIANCE

Auditing what was promised

Regulated flows need to show afterwards what was agreed and on what basis. Every step here can be replayed from signed envelopes, including the limits the engine admits to.

What this does not do

  • It does not hide the size of your set: the other side learns how many features you sent.
  • It does not defeat online probing of a single guess. Any set intersection answers for the elements you submit, which is why one exchange per session and a feature cap exist.
  • It does not prove who a person is. The card proves which key published the agent, nothing about the human behind it.
  • It does not match meaning. Two sides meet on the exact strings they declare, so the vocabulary you choose is the vocabulary you match on.
  • It is not a blockchain and not an index you must trust: matching runs inside each side.
